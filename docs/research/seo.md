# TinyBooth SEO Strategy

Last updated: 2026-04-26
Owner: Camrynn

## Executive summary

TinyBooth is going after two related but distinct search markets: people looking for a software photobooth for their iPad (TinyBooth, the app) and people looking for a way to collect photos from guests at a party (TinyWall, the photo wall). Both markets have real demand, well-known competitors, and clear keyword targets. Neither has a dominant brand that owns the conversation, which is good for us.

Key takeaways:

1. **Use subdirectories, not subdomains.** Move `wall.tinybooth.com` to `tinybooth.com/wall` and put the dashboard at `tinybooth.com/dashboard`. Subdirectories pass link equity to the root domain and rank faster. The main competitors (Simple Booth, Snappic, Pixelparty, Kululu) all do this. Sources back it up: Backlinko, Semrush, multiple case studies showing 15 to 45 percent traffic lifts after subdomain to subdirectory migrations.
2. **The party-wall side is the easier early win.** "QR code wedding photos" is pulling 2,600 monthly searches per Pov.camera's published data, and the top results are mostly small brands with thin content. We can outrank them with one strong landing page plus 5 to 8 supporting blog posts.
3. **The photobooth-app side is more competitive but the head term gets real volume.** "Photo booth app" and "photobooth app" together pull an estimated 8,000 to 15,000 monthly US searches based on the listicles ranking for them. Simple Booth dominates the SERP with high-quality long-form content. Beating them takes a real content effort.
4. **The biggest near-term lever is the App Store, not Google.** The iOS App Store has its own search engine and TinyBooth already has a 5-year-old App Store listing. Fixing the title, subtitle, keywords field, and screenshots is cheaper than ranking on Google and converts better.
5. **One brand, one site.** Right now `tinybooth.com` resolves to nothing and `wall.tinybooth.com` is its own thing. Consolidating everything under `tinybooth.com` (marketing, dashboard, wall) builds compound SEO authority and makes the cross-product story (TinyBooth + TinyWall = events) much easier to tell.

## 4. Site architecture recommendations

### Subdirectories vs subdomains: pick subdirectories, every time

**Recommendation: serve everything off `tinybooth.com` as subdirectories.**

```
tinybooth.com/                  marketing home
tinybooth.com/wall              TinyWall product (was wall.tinybooth.com)
tinybooth.com/wall/[eventCode]  the actual guest upload page per event
tinybooth.com/app               TinyBooth iOS/Android app landing page
tinybooth.com/dashboard         logged-in event host dashboard
tinybooth.com/pricing
tinybooth.com/blog
tinybooth.com/blog/[slug]
tinybooth.com/help
```

**Why this is the right call:**

1. **Subdirectories pass link equity directly to the root domain.** Every backlink to `/blog/wedding-photo-wall-setup` strengthens `tinybooth.com/wall` and `tinybooth.com/app` indirectly. Subdomains get treated as separate sites and have to build authority from scratch. Sources: [Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/subdirectory-vs-subdomain), [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/subdomain-vs-subdirectory/), [Embarque](https://www.embarque.io/post/subdomain-vs-subdirectory).
2. **Real-world case studies show 15 to 45 percent traffic lifts from subdomain to subdirectory migrations.** G2.com had a DR 88 root domain and still spent months building trust on a `learn.g2.com` subdomain. That's the canonical cautionary tale.
3. **Google's official line ("they're equivalent") doesn't match observed behavior.** Mueller has said both are fine. Practitioners consistently see subdirectories outperform. We should follow what works in the wild, not the official PR line.
4. **Operationally, it's also simpler.** One Vercel project, one analytics property, one Search Console property, one schema configuration, one robots.txt, one sitemap.

### Should we move wall.tinybooth.com to tinybooth.com/wall?

**Yes. Plan the migration carefully but do it.**

Migration checklist:

1. **Inventory existing URLs on wall.tinybooth.com.** Export from Vercel logs, the existing Postgres DB (event codes), and Google Search Console if it's set up. Goal: a complete list of every URL that has ever been served.
2. **Build the new tinybooth.com site with `/wall` paths first.** Don't redirect yet. Confirm the new structure works for new and existing event codes.
3. **Set up 301 redirects from `wall.tinybooth.com/*` to `tinybooth.com/wall/*`.** Use exact-match redirects per URL, not a wildcard, where possible. Wildcard fallback for unmatched paths.
4. **Update DNS to keep wall.tinybooth.com pointing somewhere that issues redirects** (Vercel can do this directly; just keep the old project alive long enough to redirect, or run a tiny redirect-only deployment).
5. **Submit the new sitemap to Google Search Console** and add a Change of Address request for the subdomain.
6. **Update internal links and any external links you control** (App Store description, social bios, anywhere wall.tinybooth.com appears).
7. **Monitor for 4-6 weeks.** Expect a temporary 10 to 30 percent organic traffic dip during reindexing, then recovery and growth. Source: [Aleyda Solis](https://www.aleydasolis.com/htaccess-redirects-generator/subdomain-to-subdirectory/), [Flyrank](https://www.flyrank.com/blogs/seo-hub/how-to-migrate-a-subdomain-with-proper-redirection).

Realistically, since wall.tinybooth.com was a one-event proof of concept with almost no organic backlinks or search authority to begin with, the migration risk is essentially zero. We're doing it for the future, not protecting existing rankings.

### App Store Optimization (ASO)

Apple has its own search engine and it has more direct revenue impact than Google for an app business. Treat it as a separate but parallel SEO effort.

Per [ASOMobile](https://asomobile.net/en/blog/aso-in-2026-the-complete-guide-to-app-optimization/) and [AppLaunchFlow](https://www.applaunchflow.com/blog/aso-2026-guide):

**iOS App Store fields and recommendations:**

| Field | Limit | Recommendation for TinyBooth |
|---|---|---|
| App Name | 30 chars | `TinyBooth: Photo Booth App` (26 chars) |
| Subtitle | 30 chars | `Free iPad Booth for Parties` (27 chars) |
| Keywords field (hidden) | 100 chars | `wedding,event,party,photostrip,ipad,iphone,airprint,selfie,strip,layout,birthday,booth` |
| Promotional text | 170 chars | Update for seasonal campaigns. "New: 360 layouts and party-wall sync. Free, no account needed." |
| Description | 4000 chars | Lead paragraph hits the keywords naturally. Include feature bullets. End with link to TinyWall |

Rules per Apple's ASO docs:

- Don't repeat words across name, subtitle, keywords field. Apple counts each keyword once. Plurals usually don't need a separate keyword.
- Skip stop words ("the", "for", "a", "and", etc.) in the keywords field.
- Apple now uses OCR on screenshot text. Caption screenshots with keyword-rich descriptions like "Print classic 1x4 photo strips" instead of vague slogans.

**Screenshot order (most important to least):**

1. Hero shot: an iPad on a stand at an actual event with the app open
2. Photo strip layout selector (showing 1x4, 2x2, single, etc.)
3. The random message feature (TinyBooth's signature)
4. AirPrint to a real printer
5. Event branding / theme colors
6. Free vs Pro feature comparison

**Google Play notes:**

- Google Play indexes the full description, so keyword density (used naturally) matters more there than on iOS.
- Use the same app name and subtitle approach but you have 80 characters for the title.
- Add the in-app purchase products with clear names. "Remove Watermark" is more searchable than "Pro Unlock".

## 6. Technical SEO musts

### Next.js 16 specific (assuming we're on App Router)

- **Use `generateMetadata` for every page** that has dynamic content. Static `metadata` exports for static pages.
- **Default to SSG for marketing pages.** Blog posts, product pages, landing pages should all be statically generated. Use ISR if content changes occasionally.
- **SSR (or RSC streaming) for the dashboard.** It's behind auth and not indexed, so SEO doesn't apply. Optimize for fast TTFB.
- **The per-event TinyWall upload page should be dynamic, no-index.** Each event's URL is unique to the host; don't expose it to search.
- **Use Next.js's built-in `sitemap.ts` and `robots.ts`.** They emit `/sitemap.xml` and `/robots.txt` automatically. ([Next.js metadata docs](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/file-conventions/metadata)).
- **Use `next/image` everywhere.** Photobooth content is image-heavy. Image optimization is the single biggest LCP lever you have.
- **Use `next/font` for any custom font.** Avoid CLS from font swaps.

### Structured data (JSON-LD, server-rendered)

Per [Google's structured data docs](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data) and [Search Engine Land's 2026 update on FAQ schema](https://searchengineland.com/faq-schema-rise-fall-seo-today-463993):

- **`SoftwareApplication` / `MobileApplication` schema** on the app product page. Include `applicationCategory: "PhotoApplication"`, `operatingSystem: "iOS, Android"`, `offers` (free), `aggregateRating` if you have App Store ratings to surface.
- **`Product` schema with `Offer`** on the pricing page for paid tiers.
- **`Organization` schema** in the root layout: name, logo, sameAs (links to App Store, Play Store, social).
- **`BreadcrumbList` schema** on every nested page.
- **`Article` schema** on blog posts. Include `author`, `datePublished`, `dateModified`.
- **`Review` schema** if you publish individual customer reviews on a page (don't fake them).
- **Skip `FAQPage` schema for the rich result.** Google restricted FAQ rich results to government and health sites in 2023. The schema doesn't hurt but it won't show in SERPs. Focus FAQ-style content on covering long-tail informational queries naturally.
- **Skip `HowTo` schema.** Google removed those rich results too.

### Core Web Vitals targets

Per [web.dev Vitals docs](https://web.dev/articles/vitals) and [the 2026 update](https://www.corewebvitals.io/core-web-vitals):

| Metric | Target (75th percentile) |
|---|---|
| LCP (Largest Contentful Paint) | under 2.5s |
| INP (Interaction to Next Paint) | under 200ms |
| CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift) | under 0.1 |

Concrete tactics for hitting these:

- Preload the LCP image on the home page (typically the hero shot).
- Use `next/image` with `priority` on above-the-fold images.
- Avoid client-side data fetching for above-the-fold content. Render server-side.
- No layout shifts from late-loading images: always specify width/height (or use `fill` with a sized container).
- Avoid third-party scripts on the marketing site. If you must include them (analytics, intercom, etc.), use `next/script` with `strategy="afterInteractive"` or `lazyOnload`.
- Test with [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/) and [WebPageTest](https://www.webpagetest.org/) before launch.

### Internationalization

- Start English-only.
- Reserve `tinybooth.com/[locale]/` URL patterns from day one even if only `/en/` is implemented (or default to no locale prefix for English). Don't lock yourself out.
- When you add Spanish, French, etc., use `hreflang` tags via `generateMetadata`. Sub-folders (`tinybooth.com/es/`) again, not subdomains.

### Other technical hygiene

- **Verify in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools** day one of production.
- **Submit sitemap.xml to both** as soon as the site is live.
- **Set canonical URLs everywhere.** Especially important when filter / category pages on the blog could create duplicate content.
- **Use proper status codes.** 404 for missing pages, 410 for deliberately removed content, 301 for permanent moves.
- **Add OpenGraph and Twitter Card meta tags** for social shares. Default OG image per page is fine; custom OG images for blog posts is better.

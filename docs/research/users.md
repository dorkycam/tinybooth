# User Research: TinyBooth + TinyWall

## Executive summary

Two clear personas emerge. The DIY photobooth host is almost always running an iPad on a stand at a wedding, birthday, sweet 16, or quinceañera. They picked the app route because pro photobooth rental quotes came in at $550 to $1,100 for three hours, and they realized the same money buys an iPad they get to keep. They want a setup that survives 150 guests, prints to a Canon Selphy, and does not crash. The QR-photo-wall host is usually a bride, groom, or birthday host who got burned by the death of WedPics, the dead wedding hashtag, and the friction of asking 150 people to download anything. They want a single QR code that anyone (including grandma) can scan, photos that go straight to a private gallery they own, and a live slideshow on the venue TV. Both groups share the same three deepest fears: the app crashing mid-event, photos getting lost forever, and looking cheap or broken in front of guests. They will pay to remove that fear. They will not pay for a subscription they have to remember to cancel.

## Group 1: Photobooth app users

### Who they are

- DIY brides and grooms running a photobooth at their own reception (most common).
- Birthday party hosts (sweet 16, quinceañera, milestone birthdays).
- Casual home use (kids' parties, holiday gatherings, classroom use).
- A small but loud group of semi-pro operators who do this as a side hustle.

### Why they DIY instead of renting

The numbers do the talking. The U.S. average for a 3-hour photo booth rental is **$550 to $1,100**, with open-air booths averaging $870 and 360 booths averaging $1,170 ([Puddles Photo Booth, 2025](https://www.puddlesphotobooth.com/2025-photo-booth-rental-costs)). Add-ons push that up by $150 to $800 ([same source](https://www.puddlesphotobooth.com/2025-photo-booth-rental-costs)).

Compare that to a DIY stack: an iPad (already owned or a $329 base model), a $20 to $60 app, a $99 to $159 Canon Selphy CP1300/CP1500 printer, a $40 ring light, and a backdrop. Total all-in: roughly $300 to $500, and the host owns the gear.

**Representative quotes:**

- "Downloaded this app to have a budget Photo Booth experience for a party of ~150 people, and it worked out perfectly." ([LumaBooth App Store review](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/lumabooth-event-photo-booth/id1162206015))
- "This was the perfect alternative to booking a photo booth at our wedding!" ([LumaBooth App Store review](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/lumabooth-event-photo-booth/id1162206015))
- One bride bought a Canon Selphy from Best Buy "for a fraction of what a photobooth would cost" and pre-cut craft paper for guest message books ([WeddingWire DIY thread](https://www.weddingwire.com/wedding-forums/diy-ipad-photobooth/d7089aea51be97f4.html)).

### Top features they wish existed

Pulled directly from App Store reviews, weddingbee, weddingwire, theKnot, and review aggregator forums.

1. **Reliability over flash.** Every app has a story of crashing 30 minutes into an event. "Insufficient system resources or software conflicts" comes up repeatedly ([troubleshooting guides](https://help.simplebooth.com/en/articles/6047032-quick-troubleshooting-guide)).
2. **Snap-to-corner print layout editing.** "The print layout is extremely finicky, with resizing photos being difficult to line up" ([dslrBooth review](https://dslrbooth.com/dslrbooth-reviews)). LumaBooth users want the same.
3. **Per-photo selection from a strip.** A common WeddingBee complaint: "Wedding Booth app only saves the final layout so you can't pull your favorite from the set of 3 to print, send, etc." ([Weddingbee Boards](https://boards.weddingbee.com/topic/ipad-wedding-photobooth-any-experiencesuggestions/)).
4. **Reprint after the fact.** "No going back, no reprinting, no remaining, no second text send" once the guest moves past the screen ([Simple Booth Classic review](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/simple-booth-classic/id697826715)).
5. **A real countdown.** Pocketbooth's red/green light system gets specifically called out as confusing: users requested an on-screen countdown for years and were told no on purpose ([Pocketbooth reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pocketbooth-photo-booth/id385145330)).
6. **Front-facing camera that actually works.** Pocketbooth users paid $4.99 for a Party Pack expecting front camera and got nothing ([same source](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pocketbooth-photo-booth/id385145330)).
7. **Brand/event customization without a website.** Hosts want to set the colors, add their names, and pick a layout in the app, not be sent to a portal.
8. **Unattended mode.** A real complaint about Simple Booth: a previous version let you disable the "Next" button so guests just took photos and walked away. Newer version makes you tap through ([Simple Booth Classic review](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/simple-booth-classic/id697826715)).
9. **Guided Access friendliness.** Setup tutorials universally tell hosts to enable iOS Guided Access so drunk guests can't escape the app ([Joy Factory blog](https://blog.thejoyfactory.com/how-to-setup-a-mobile-tablet-photo-booth)).
10. **Lighting helper.** Multiple hosts mention "buy a lamp" as essential. An in-app brightness/exposure check before the event would cut a real support load.

### Top complaints with current apps

**Pocketbooth (the legacy classic):**

- "The new version deleted all the in app purchases I had brought." ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pocketbooth-photo-booth/id385145330))
- "you have to jump through so many hoops just to take a photo!"
- "I don't want to create events to use it"
- "It's no longer special or unique. It's not nostalgic"
- "Since each photo is so small on the strip it also doesn't make for high quality"

**LumaBooth (the iPad workhorse):**

- Subscription frustration: "Couldn't find a way to contact support despite having paid the $20 subscription fee, with the party coming that weekend" ([App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/lumabooth-event-photo-booth/id1162206015)).
- QR code generation lag (~2 seconds) gets flagged in reviews.
- Boomerang/video upload failures.
- App crashes when an event library gets large.

**dslrBooth/LumaBooth (Mac/Win):**

- "Not much thought was given to the user experience for the operator. Especially clear in the 'Advanced Settings' window" ([dslrBooth reviews](https://dslrbooth.com/dslrbooth-reviews)).
- No phone support, only email.
- Camera compatibility surprises (Sony A6400 didn't work for one user who had to rent a Canon).

**Simple Booth Classic ($59.99 one-time, no longer maintained as the flagship):**

- One business user paid $80, then was told the data capture they expected was an "enterprise" feature for $199/month ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/simple-booth-classic/id697826715)).
- After printer failures, "no going back" to recover the photo.

**Cross-cutting:**

- Wifi printing over AirPrint to a Canon Selphy is fragile. After 8 to 10 prints the queue stalls and the iPad needs a full power cycle ([Wifibooth forum](https://wifibooth.com/community/viewtopic.php?t=2159)).
- Venue wifi gets oversaturated when 150 guests connect ([printing guide](https://www.simplebooth.com/blog/wifi-photo-booth-printing/)).

### What makes them pay vs stay free

**Pay triggers, in order of how often they show up:**

1. Removing watermarks before a real event (the strongest single trigger).
2. Unlocking SMS/email send-to-guest.
3. Custom branding (event name, colors, logo on strip).
4. Higher print quality / more layouts.
5. Removing ads on free apps.

**Free-tier expectations:**

Casual users will not pay anything for "just take a goofy strip at home." Mini Photobooth at 4.7 stars and free-with-IAP shows the floor. The moment it becomes "an event we're hosting" the wallet opens.

The clearest gap: **per-event pricing**. Snappic offers $19/event. Most one-time-per-event hosts hate signing up for a subscription they have to cancel. This is a real opportunity.

### How important is printing? What printers do people actually use?

Printing is **the** physical artifact people remember. Not optional for the wedding/event use case.

- **Canon Selphy CP1300/CP1500** dominates DIY. Cheap (~$129 to $179), AirPrint compatible, dye-sub quality. TikTok and weddingbee threads are full of these ([TikTok DIY photobooth Selphy](https://www.tiktok.com/@mrslincoln_/video/7293868025768348960), [weddingbee](https://boards.weddingbee.com/topic/ipad-wedding-photobooth-any-experiencesuggestions/)).
- **DNP DS620A and DNP RX1** are the pro choice (12-second prints, 700 sheet capacity), but cost ~$1,000+ and need a Mac/PC bridge to reach iPad ([Simple Booth printer guide](https://www.simplebooth.com/blog/best-photo-booth-printers/)).
- **Canon Selphy quote**: "amazing quality... I use it to print out little photo albums for clients because it's that good" (Reddit photographer).

**Standard print sizes:**

- **2x6 strip**: classic, 4 photos vertical, two strips per 4x6 sheet ([weddingbee](https://boards.weddingbee.com/topic/photobooth-4x6-vs-2x6-strip/)).
- **4x6 postcard**: single big photo or 2x2 collage, fits any standard photo printer.

### Tablet vs phone, the real-world setup

iPad wins, decisively.

- "The big screen of a tablet provides guests with a classic, immersive photo booth experience and makes it super easy for groups to frame themselves" ([Fun Event App](https://funeventapp.com/tablet-photo-booth-5-reasons-to-use-them/)).
- Standard setup at every wedding thread: iPad on a tall light stand, ring light, Canon Selphy on a side table, backdrop behind, near a power outlet, with iOS Guided Access on.
- Phones get used only for casual home use or when the host literally has no iPad.
- Android tablet support is asked for but rarely delivered well. "With the same features as an iPad, you can get an Android tablet at half the price" ([Fun Event App](https://funeventapp.com/tablet-photo-booth-5-reasons-to-use-them/)).
- Landscape vs portrait: weddings prefer **portrait** orientation for the iPad on a stand because it matches the strip output and feels like a classic booth. Casual selfie use is portrait too.

### Setup pain points (huge opportunity)

These come up in basically every DIY thread:

- "Buy a lamp or spotlight to use as lighting" ([weddingbee](https://boards.weddingbee.com/topic/ipad-wedding-photobooth-any-experiencesuggestions/)).
- "Make sure your booth is near a power outlet... turn off the lock/off screen function" ([same source](https://boards.weddingbee.com/topic/ipad-wedding-photobooth-any-experiencesuggestions/)).
- "Assign someone to monitor booth every 30 minutes."
- "Lacking venue internet prevented printing photos" — showing why offline printing must work.

## Group 2: QR photo-wall hosts

### Who they are

- Brides and grooms (the dominant cohort).
- Milestone birthday hosts (30s, 40s, 50s).
- Bridal shower / engagement / rehearsal dinner hosts.
- Corporate event organizers (smaller share but higher willingness to pay).

### Why they pick QR-wall over Instagram hashtags

The wedding hashtag is dead. Reasons cited everywhere:

- "By the time the dance floor opens, most people are thinking about refilling their drink, not remembering your hashtag" ([Snapseek blog](https://snapseek.app/blog/why-wedding-hashtags-are-dead)).
- Hashtags are **public**. "When you use a hashtag, you are splashing your event across the public internet, but weddings are personal" ([Snapseek](https://snapseek.app/blog/why-wedding-hashtags-are-dead)).
- Algorithms bury you. "If your beautiful portrait doesn't get 500 likes instantly, it gets buried at the bottom of the feed" ([same source](https://snapseek.app/blog/why-wedding-hashtags-are-dead)).
- People keep favorites on their camera roll and never post. "Many guests keep their favorite shots on their camera roll, share them privately in group chats, or post them to Stories that vanish after 24 hours."
- Compression. iMessage/WhatsApp/Instagram all crush quality.

**Direct user voice:**

- "guests scan it to upload photos and videos... even my grandma figured it out" — knottie02c88b60f5dbd041 ([theKnot forum](https://forums.theknot.com/discussion/1087842/qr-code-app-for-photos)).
- "We got 200+ photos back the same night." — knottie63f30bba8cc03c6d ([same](https://forums.theknot.com/discussion/1087842/qr-code-app-for-photos)).
- "I got 850 photos and videos, so it was worth it." — knottie7411422a0fed7d0c ([same](https://forums.theknot.com/discussion/1087842/qr-code-app-for-photos)).
- "I don't need another social media app on my phone." — kimmiinthemitten ([theKnot](https://forums.theknot.com/discussion/1069875/photo-sharing-apps)).
- "I'm not going to download an app for one event." — photokitty (moderator on theKnot).
- "I downloaded WedPics for a friends wedding the day before and deleted it the day after." — kimmiinthemitten ([same](https://forums.theknot.com/discussion/1069875/photo-sharing-apps)).
- "Your photographer can't be everywhere — this gave us so many angles and candid photos we wouldn't have gotten (500+)! And no waiting to see it" — Stacy ([Kululu](https://www.kululu.com/wedding-photo-sharing-app)).

That last one captures the deepest emotional driver: **the photographer can't be everywhere**. Couples want the cousin's photo of grandma crying that the pro photographer missed.

### How they discover these services

In rough order:

1. **TheKnot, WeddingWire, Reddit (r/weddingplanning, r/wedding) word of mouth.** Every thread is "what did you use, was it worth it."
2. **TikTok creators** showing DIY wedding tech setups.
3. **Pinterest** boards titled "wedding tech" or "wedding photo wall."
4. **Wedding planner referrals** for higher-budget events.
5. **Google search** for "wedding photo sharing app" or "QR code wedding photos." This is where SEO matters most.
6. **Instagram ads** from competitors like POV, Kululu, GuestPix.

### Their #1 fear: photos lost, inappropriate uploads, or the tech failing live

All three fears come up. WedPics shutting down in February 2019 created a generational scar. "Users were given three weeks to download all of their full resolution photos and videos before they were no longer accessible" ([The Dead Pixels Society](https://thedeadpixelssociety.com/wedpics-to-shut-down-feb-15/)). Couples lost wedding memories. This is brought up over and over in current threads.

**Lost-photos fear** ranks first. Every paid platform now leads marketing with "you own your data," "12 months of access," "download as ZIP," "stays in your Google Drive."

**Inappropriate-photo fear** is real but usually overestimated. Vendors say "in 1000+ events, guests generally do behave" ([Fotify](https://fotify.app/blog/best-wedding-photo-sharing-apps-2026/)), but the moderation feature still sells. Solutions: AI screening (Fotify charges $29.99 for it), manual approval before public-screen display, hide from slideshow but show in private gallery.

**Tech failing live** is the most acute on-the-day fear. Specifically:

- "Performance hiccups under load (slow uploads at peak moments)" ([wedpicsqr blog](https://www.wedpicsqr.com/blog/wedding-photo-sharing-apps-vs-qr-codes-which-to-choose)).
- "Apps need 4+ Mbps upload speed to work well, while QR codes work with basic cell service" ([same](https://www.wedpicsqr.com/blog/wedding-photo-sharing-apps-vs-qr-codes-which-to-choose)).
- "QR codes can be finicky and less pleasant for those less tech-savvy" — banana468 ([theKnot](https://forums.theknot.com/discussion/1087842/qr-code-app-for-photos)).

### How long do they want photo access after the event

The market settled on **12 months** as the standard.

- GuestPix: 12 months from event date, renewable ([GuestPix pricing](https://guestpix.com/weddings-pricing/)).
- GuestCam: 12 months ([honcho roundup](https://thehoncho.app/blog/the-8-best-wedding-photo-sharing-apps/)).
- Guestlense: 3-month upload window, 12-month download window ([same](https://thehoncho.app/blog/the-8-best-wedding-photo-sharing-apps/)).
- Lense: 12-month storage ([Lense](https://lense.app/disposable-camera-app-for-weddings)).
- Wedibox: open until you close it.
- Kululu: 7 or 30-day upload window (lower tier), longer for paid.

The free-tier floor is roughly **7 to 30 days**. Paid tier expects 12 months. A 90-day archive option is also offered (Chivent's Premium Pass is $16.99 with 10GB storage and 90-day archival).

### Custom branding, how much do they care

**A lot for weddings, less for casual parties.**

- GuestPix charges $40 more (Classic $49 → Signature $89) just to add custom colors and fonts ([pricing page](https://guestpix.com/weddings-pricing/)).
- "Customization is good but not limitless" comes up as a Wedibox complaint ([honcho](https://thehoncho.app/blog/the-8-best-wedding-photo-sharing-apps/)).
- The branding hosts care most about: their names/event title on the QR landing page, their event colors on the slideshow, a custom QR code design, and a printable QR sign template.
- Hosts do **not** care about deeply customizing the UI of the upload flow. They just want it to feel theirs at the touchpoints (sign, gallery header, slideshow).

### Price sensitivity, what range feels reasonable per event

**Sweet spot: $30 to $90 per event.** Above $100 only the wedding tier converts.

Real prices being charged:

- WedUploader: $39 one-time ([honcho](https://thehoncho.app/blog/the-8-best-wedding-photo-sharing-apps/)).
- GuestPix: $49 / $89 / $119 tiers ([GuestPix](https://guestpix.com/weddings-pricing/)).
- Kululu: $39 to $99+ ([web search](https://eventsnap.co/blog/best-free-qr-code-photo-sharing-apps-2026)).
- EventSnap: $9.99 single event or $14.99/month.
- Guest.Gallery: starts $47/event.
- Chivent Premium: $16.99 starter.
- POV: free under 10 guests, paid above.

**Absolute red flags for buyers:**

- Subscriptions you have to remember to cancel ("Hate that Guestpix doesn't have a free trial" — knottie7411422a0fed7d0c).
- Per-photo pricing.
- Pay-to-download-your-own-photos.
- Hidden upload limits that surprise the host mid-event.

**What unlocks the wallet:**

- Confirmed unlimited uploads.
- Live slideshow on the venue TV.
- 12 months of access.
- ZIP download of everything.
- Custom QR signage / printable templates.

### What features they actually want from a wall service

Synthesized across forums:

- **No-app, no-account guest upload.** Period. Non-negotiable. Even adding a name field has friction.
- **Live slideshow on the TV** that updates in real time as photos come in. This is the magic moment. ([Kululu testimonial](https://www.kululu.com/wedding-photo-sharing-app): "The photo slideshow was a hit among our guests").
- **Both photos and videos.** "pov doesn't let you upload videos" was a specific complaint that drove a couple to switch ([theKnot](https://forums.theknot.com/discussion/1087842/qr-code-app-for-photos)).
- **Reminder pings after the event** so people who didn't upload at the venue still upload later. Dots Memories sends these.
- **Face recognition / "find me" search** is a wow feature on GuestCam but not yet table stakes.
- **AI moderation toggle** for hosts who are nervous.
- **Printable QR signage** that matches the event style.
- **Voice messages / digital guestbook** as a bonus that several apps now bundle.
- **Disposable-camera-style limit per guest** (Lense's whole pitch). Some hosts love this for the curation effect, most weddings prefer unlimited.

## Implications for TinyBooth/TinyWall

These are concrete product decisions backed by the research above.

1. **TinyBooth must default to portrait, iPad-first, with a Guided-Access-friendly UI.** Every successful DIY photobooth setup is a portrait iPad on a tall light stand. Build for that as the default, then make landscape and phone work. Never design phone-first.

2. **AirPrint to Canon Selphy CP1300/CP1500 is the single most important hardware integration.** It is the printer DIY hosts already own or buy. Test against it religiously. Implement a print queue that recovers from the known iOS Print Center stall after 8 to 10 prints (auto-restart the queue, surface the issue to the operator with one-tap "fix it" instead of requiring an iPad reboot). This alone will beat every competitor on the print reliability dimension.

3. **Per-event pricing beats subscriptions for our target user.** Snappic's $19-per-event option proves the demand. Offer pay-per-event credits ($19 to $29 per event) alongside an optional unlimited-events subscription for semi-pros. Most one-and-done wedding hosts hate the cancel-the-sub friction.

4. **Free TinyBooth must be genuinely usable, with watermark + capped event size as the only limits.** The current free Pocketbooth-style users are vocal when features get gated. Free tier: unlimited photos, all layouts, all messages, AirPrint to any printer, watermark on the strip and on the IG share, soft cap on simultaneous send-to-guest sends per event. Paid removes watermark and unlocks event branding, custom messages, and dashboard.

5. **Always allow reprint and per-photo pull from a strip.** Two of the loudest complaints from Pocketbooth, Simple Booth, and Wedding Booth users: no reprint after the fact, and no way to pull a single shot from a strip. Both are easy wins.

6. **Build a real on-screen countdown with a personality.** Pocketbooth's refusal to add one is a 10-year unresolved complaint. Make it big, friendly, customizable per event. Keep an "old-school no-countdown" toggle for the purists.

7. **For TinyWall, the QR-to-upload flow must be one tap, no name field by default.** Add an optional "who are you" field for hosts who want it. Apple App Clips on iOS and instant web on Android, no install ([POV does this](https://pov.camera/)). Test on grandma's iPhone 8 and a 5-year-old Android in low-signal venue conditions.

8. **TinyWall pricing: free tier with watermark + 7-day retention, then a $39 to $89 per-event tier matching market norms.** Free tier should be enough to actually run a small birthday. Paid tier unlocks 12-month retention, custom QR signage, custom branding/colors, AI moderation toggle, ZIP download, live slideshow theme. Above $89 is a "luxe" tier with multiple events bundled (matches GuestPix Luxe at $119 for 3 galleries).

9. **Address the WedPics graveyard fear directly in marketing.** Promise: "Download all your photos as a ZIP at any time. We'll never delete your event without 90 days notice and one-click full export." This is a trust lever every existing competitor underplays.

10. **Live slideshow on the venue TV is a must-have, not a nice-to-have.** Every Kululu, Wedibox, Fotify, Instant Spotlight testimonial features it. Build a clean web-based slideshow at wall.tinybooth.com/slideshow/[event] that runs on any smart TV browser, with theme matching the event branding. This is the moment guests gasp.

11. **Inappropriate-content moderation is a sell-able peace-of-mind feature, but should be opt-in.** AI moderation as a paid add-on (matches Fotify at $29.99). Default behavior: photo lands in private gallery instantly, but the host can toggle "approve before showing on the slideshow" with one tap.

12. **Cross-product event branding is the moat.** No competitor unifies a photobooth app and a guest-upload wall under one event. TinyBooth strips and TinyWall slideshow share the same colors, logo, and event title. This is the genuinely new product.

13. **Reminder push to guests who haven't uploaded** (TinyWall premium feature, low-cost: a single SMS or web notification 24 to 48 hours after the event). Dots Memories does this and gets specifically called out as "10/10 highly recommend." Easy implementation, real perceived value.

14. **No subscription auto-renewal traps. Honor refunds aggressively.** GuestPix offers a 14-day money-back guarantee. Match it. The category's biggest complaint cohort is "I paid for a thing and it failed at my event." Removing that fear converts.

15. **Marketing must answer "is this the next WedPics" within the first scroll on tinybooth.com.** Simple promises: independent (not VC-funded), photos exportable any time, free tier you can keep using forever, transparent pricing, no surprise paywalls. This positions us against every burned-out incumbent.
